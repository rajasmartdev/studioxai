How Diabetes Affects Your Nerves

Understanding the progression helps you act at the right time

Step 01
High Blood Sugar
Persistently elevated glucose damages blood vessels supplying nerves
Step 02
Reduced Sensation Develops
Nerve fibres begin to lose their ability to transmit signals properly
Step 03
Minor Injuries Go Unnoticed
Small cuts, blisters, or pressure injuries are not felt due to numbness
Step 04
Risk of Wounds & Complications
Untreated injuries can lead to ulcers, infections, and serious outcomes
Early nerve evaluation can break this cycle. Detecting neuropathy before complications arise is the most effective way to protect your long-term mobility and health.

You May Benefit From A Neuropathy Evaluation If You:

Have diabetes for more than 5 years
Experience burning or tingling sensations in the feet
Have numbness in feet or toes
Have balance issues or unsteadiness while walking
Have a history of foot ulcers or slow-healing wounds
Have uncontrolled blood sugar levels (HbA1c > 7%)

If any of the above apply to you, a neuropathy assessment is recommended.

Common Questions

Diabetic neuropathy is caused by prolonged high blood sugar levels that damage the nerves throughout the body. The most common type affects the legs and feet. Factors like poor blood sugar control, high blood pressure, and duration of diabetes all contribute to the risk.
Neuropathy is diagnosed through a combination of clinical examination, symptom history, and nerve function tests. At Boston Diabetes, we use the DPN Check device for quantitative nerve conduction assessment — providing objective, measurable results rather than relying on subjective testing alone.
