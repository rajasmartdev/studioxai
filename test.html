Clinical evidence

What the studies show.

NC-stat DPNCheck and its predecessor device NC-stat have been studied in independent peer-reviewed research published since 2006. Every figure on this page carries a numbered citation, and every citation names the reference standard the figure was measured against.

How results are reported

NC-stat DPNCheck reports sural sensory nerve action potential amplitude and sensory nerve conduction velocity. Results are compared against normal limits that the site configures at setup. The default is an age and height adjusted limit set drawn from the published normative monograph, and fixed and custom limit sets are also available.3 Results outside the configured limits are reported as abnormal. Interpretation is made by the clinician.

Why no single threshold appears on this page.
Because the normal limits are configurable and the default set adjusts for age and height, no single pair of values is correct for every patient. Read your configured limits from your own software settings and from the normative monograph supplied with the device.

On monitoring change over time.
The authors of reference 5 concluded that their reliability results question the suitability of the device for monitoring the progression of diabetic peripheral neuropathy, and called for a longer, pre-specified, fully crossed study. Alera publishes that conclusion alongside the reliability figures because it bears directly on how the device should and should not be used.

About

Innovation with purpose.

Alera Medtech works on devices that sit underneath daily care in neurology and vascular access. Needle and surface electrodes, intraoperative monitoring, point-of-care nerve conduction, catheter securement. High volume, routine, and largely unchanged for years.

The problem we work on

These devices are bought by the case and used without much thought, which is exactly why they are worth redesigning. A small improvement in a device used ten thousand times a week compounds. Our work is modern engineering and materials applied to products the market has stopped paying attention to, protected by intellectual property that reflects an actual functional advance rather than a filing exercise.

What we hold ourselves to

Simplicity

A device should be obvious in the hand. If it needs explaining at the bedside, it is not finished.

Patient first

Safety and comfort decide the design when they conflict with cost or speed.

Intellectual property with substance

We file on functional advances, not on decoration.

Access

A better device that a health system cannot afford to standardize on has not improved anything.

How we work with clinicians

We design with the people who use these devices, and we publish what the evidence actually says, including where it is limited. Clinicians, researchers and health systems interested in evaluation or collaboration are welcome to get in touch.
