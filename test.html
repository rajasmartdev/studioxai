A System, Not a Setting

The forest at Kanthavara is not a backdrop for activities. It is the subject of all learning here.

Spanning 13 acres of protected rainforest within Karnataka's Western Ghats region, this ecosystem demonstrates biodiversity, habitat interdependence, and the consequences of both degradation and regeneration.

Visitors do not come here to see the forest. They come to understand it.

Living Biodiversity

Rainforest Ecosystem

Wildlife Residents

  • Leopards (resident population, camera trap documented)
  • Wild boar
  • Porcupine
  • Diverse bird species
  • Butterfly populations
  • Amphibians and reptiles
  • Insect diversity

Flora Diversity

Native rainforest species including medicinal plants, fruiting trees, and undergrowth supporting the complete food web. The arboretum preserves significant specimens as living gene bank.

Ecosystem Services

The forest demonstrates:

  • Water cycle function (rainfall patterns, groundwater recharge)
  • Atmospheric cooling
  • Carbon sequestration
  • Soil stabilization
  • Habitat connectivity
Wildlife sightings are not guaranteed. These are wild animals in natural habitat, not exhibits. Learning to read signs of presence is more valuable than viewing.

68 Varieties of Green Gold

Kanthavara's bambusetum contains 68 bamboo varieties — nearly half of India's 150 species. This is one of the most comprehensive bamboo collections in the region, serving both conservation and educational purposes.

Notable Specimens:

  • World's second-largest bamboo (reaching 100 feet in 30-40 days)
  • World's smallest bamboo variety
  • Black bamboo
  • Blue bamboo
  • Wine bamboo
  • Creeper/climbing bamboo varieties

Why Bamboo Matters:

  • Generates 30% more oxygen than equivalent tree coverage
  • Some species grow up to 4 feet (1.2 metres) per day
  • Root systems function as water conduits
  • Provides temperature cooling effect
  • Sustainable material for construction, textiles, food
  • Critical habitat component in Asian forests

The bambusetum serves as primary teaching resource for understanding sustainable applications and bamboo's role in forest ecosystems.

Living Gene Bank

Arboretum

Curated collection of native and significant tree species from the Western Ghats region. The arboretum serves dual purpose:

Conservation: Preserving genetic diversity of native species, some increasingly rare in wild populations.

Education: Living specimens for teaching tree identification, growth patterns, ecological roles, and forestry principles.

Understanding the Water Cycle

Water Systems

Kanthavara's landscape includes:

  • 💧 Natural lakes
  • 💧 Paddy fields (integrated agriculture demonstration)
  • 💧 Rainwater harvesting systems
  • 💧 Forest streams and drainage patterns

These features demonstrate the forest's crucial role in water cycle function — how intact forest systems capture, store, filter, and release water versus degraded landscapes.

Learning from Loss

Not all of Kanthavara's 13 acres are intact forest. Sections demonstrate:

Degraded Areas

What forest loss looks like, how ecosystems collapse, what is lost beyond trees.

Regeneration Zones

How forests recover over time, what interventions help, what timelines are realistic.

Comparison Points

Side-by-side observation of healthy versus damaged forest systems.

The Broken Forest Tour uses these zones as primary teaching resource — confronting visitors with reality rather than idealized nature imagery.

Where Belief and Conservation Intersect

The land surrounding Kanthavara includes:

🛕
Temples
🌳
Sacred trees
🌿
Sacred groves (Naga Bana)
⛩️
Daiva/Bhoota shrines

These spaces have traditionally remained protected through cultural practice rather than legislation. Communities preserved biodiversity through belief systems that placed limits on exploitation.

This is not presented as spiritual tourism. It is historical documentation of how conservation functioned before formal environmental movements — and what might be learned from that relationship between belief, land, and life.
